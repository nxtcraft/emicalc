1. What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay your bank every month to repay a loan. It is "equated" because the amount stays the same every month throughout the loan tenure (for fixed-rate loans), making budgeting predictable.

Every EMI payment has two components:

  • Interest component — the cost of borrowing money, calculated on the outstanding principal
  • Principal component — the portion that actually reduces your loan balance

Here is the key insight most borrowers miss: the split between these two components changes every month. In the early years, most of your EMI goes toward interest. In the later years, most goes toward principal repayment. This is why prepayments made early in the loan tenure save far more money than the same prepayment made later.

Example: On a ₹30 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years, your EMI is ₹26,035. In Month 1, only ₹4,785 goes toward reducing your loan balance — the remaining ₹21,250 is pure interest. By Month 180 (year 15), ₹14,610 goes toward principal and only ₹11,425 to interest.

2. EMI Formula Explained (With Worked Example)

The standard EMI formula used by all Indian banks is:

EMI Formula
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)

Where:
P = Principal loan amount (₹)
r = Monthly interest rate = Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
n = Loan tenure in months

Worked Example: ₹30 Lakh Home Loan at 8.5% for 20 Years

Step 1 — Calculate monthly rate: r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083

Step 2 — Calculate tenure in months: n = 20 × 12 = 240

Step 3 — Calculate (1+r)^n: (1.007083)^240 = 5.5536

Step 4 — Apply formula:

EMI = 30,00,000 × 0.007083 × 5.5536 / (5.5536 − 1)
= 30,00,000 × 0.03934 / 4.5536
= 1,18,020 / 4.5536
= ₹26,035

6. How to Reduce Your EMI

If your EMI is straining your monthly budget, here are five proven ways to bring it down:

  1. Make a larger down payment
    Every extra rupee of down payment reduces your loan principal and therefore your EMI. Increasing your down payment from 10% to 20% on a ₹60 lakh home reduces the loan from ₹54L to ₹48L — saving ₹5,452/month in EMI.
  2. Choose a longer tenure
    Extending from 20 to 25 years on a ₹30L loan at 8.5% drops EMI from ₹26,035 to ₹24,044 — saving ₹1,991/month. The trade-off is you pay more total interest (₹42.1L vs ₹32.5L). Do this only if cash flow is tight.
  3. Balance transfer to a lower rate
    If your bank rate is 9.5% but another bank offers 8.5%, transferring a ₹30L outstanding loan saves ₹1,972/month in EMI and ₹13.6L in total interest over the remaining 20 years. Use our Balance Transfer Calculator to check your numbers.
  4. Make periodic prepayments
    When you make a prepayment, choose "Reduce Tenure" rather than "Reduce EMI" to maximise savings. If you must lower monthly outflow, choose "Reduce EMI" and your EMI drops immediately.
  5. Renegotiate your rate
    If you have been a consistent borrower for 2+ years and have a CIBIL score above 750, you can ask your bank to reduce your rate. Banks often agree rather than risk you doing a balance transfer. A 0.5% rate cut on ₹30L saves approximately ₹986/month.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed monthly payment you make to your bank to repay a loan. Each EMI has two parts: interest on the outstanding principal, and principal repayment. The ratio changes over time — early EMIs are mostly interest, later ones mostly principal.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n – 1), where P = principal, r = monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = months. For ₹30L at 8.5% for 20 years: EMI = ₹26,035. The EMICalc tool does this instantly.

Can I reduce my EMI after taking a loan?

Yes. Make a prepayment and ask the bank to reduce EMI (not tenure), do a balance transfer to a lower-rate bank, or renegotiate your rate. For floating rate loans, your EMI also adjusts when RBI changes the repo rate.

What happens if I miss an EMI payment?

You'll face a late payment penalty (1–2% of EMI), penal interest on the overdue amount, and a negative CIBIL score impact. Missing 3 consecutive EMIs can lead to the account being classified as an NPA. Contact your bank proactively if you anticipate difficulty.

Is home loan EMI tax deductible?

Yes, under the old tax regime. Interest up to ₹2L/year is deductible under Section 24(b). Principal repayment up to ₹1.5L/year is deductible under Section 80C. These deductions are not available under the new default tax regime.
